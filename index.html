Call me Jonald

Art lover and aspiring web developer

Gaming

Playing video games is, without a doubt, the thing I can do endlessly without losing interest. If I could, I'd play until my hands gave out! There's something about the adventure, the storytelling, and the worlds developers create that captivates me. If I ever get the chance to create a game people love, I'd be satisfied for life.

FF7 Rebirth was mind-blowing—a bit long-winded, but the gameplay hit all the right notes. Huge shoutout to Playstation for stepping up in the past few years, especially since the 6th-gen days when Xbox had the upper hand. While I still play most of my games on Xbox, I've spent more time on PlayStation recently, with titles like God of War, Spider-Man, Ghost of Tsushima, Horizon, and Uncharted leaving a lasting impression. It feels like PlayStation exclusives have been delivering experiences that resonate with me more.

Hogwarts Legacy felt like it should've been a PS5 exclusive—I might be imagining that, though. Despite that, Xbox still has a place in my heart for games like Star Wars, Tenkaichi, NCAA, Assassin's Creed, TMNT, and GTA. I'm expecting big things from them all. Plus, I'm hoping for some major news about the Switch 2 soon. I've recently reconnected with Pokémon, and it's been a wild ride. This might be the year I finally complete a Pokédex!

And let's be real: we've been waiting forever for Tenkaichi 4. Even though I'm not huge into fighting games, this one's going to be something special.

Cinema

Movies have always been a huge part of my life, so much so that I pay for Regal Unlimited just to catch everything I want on the big screen. While I get hyped for Marvel and DC films, ensemble casts and blockbusters like Barbie and Oppenheimer also pull me in. I've got high standards, especially when it comes to superhero movies and established universes like Star Wars, Harry Potter, and Transformers. Even when I expect certain films to disappoint, I still go for the lore. This 2024's highlights were Dune 2, Speak No Evil and Heretic definitely the best movies of the year.

That said, I miss the era of comedies like Step Brothers, Talledega Nights, and Superbad—they just don't make them like that anymore. As for superhero films, the MCU has been a bit absent in 2023, but hopefully, they'll pick up the slack soon. Sony's Spider-Verse films? Well, they've been rough lately, with misses like Venom 2 and the upcoming Kraven and Madame Web movies, which I fully expect to disappoint.

The MCU might have stumbled recently, but Captain America 4 has me excited. I'm looking forward to its spy-thriller vibe, setting up the Thunderbolts, and the introduction of adamantium. It's got the potential to restore that MCU magic. Deadpool 3 was fun with all its fan service, but it didn't do much to push the story forward. On the DC side, I'm curious about James Gunn's direction. Joker 2 was a mess, but Superman will be the real test. His decision to start Batman's story later in life is risky, but the Green Lantern castings give me hope.

Streaming

TV is similar to video games for me—something I've grown up with that gave me stories to dive into and characters to follow. From sitcoms, anime, and action-packed cartoons as a kid to superhero shows, established universes, and comedies now, it's been a constant. I still love sitcoms and anime and have developed a stronger appreciation for mysteries over time. Lately, X-Men 97, Fallout, The Boys, Supacell, and The Vince Staples Show have all delivered. I've heard there's a Dune show in the works too.

That said, TV is where the MCU started to crumble for me. The decline in quality and coherence between projects was hard to ignore. Some of that falls on Feige, but the bigger problem was overextending with too many shows and movies handled by different creators, making it tough to keep the larger story together. Revealing projects years in advance without a clear path also hurt the excitement. Sure, Agatha Harkness turned out surprisingly good, but a lot of these shows felt unnecessary. Meanwhile, Ironheart was teased and we're still waiting. X-Men 97 was fire, but it doesn't do much to help the MCU's core.

I'll admit, some fans are part of the problem. Complaining about “Marvel fatigue” is odd when there weren't 10 shows at once. If you're not into the content, fine—but missing shows and then complaining about not having all the info isn't fair to the ones keeping up. Instead of introducing new characters, I'd rather see the weaker ones from past projects improved.

Moving on from Marvel, I'm hyped for The Penguin. It's elite—serious and intense. DC is hit-or-miss, but this one's a winner. Curb Your Enthusiasm returned for its final season, and as sad as it is to see it end, I'm grateful for the laughs. RIP Richard Lewis. It's Always Sunny needs to come back with season 17 soon! And on the comedy front, The Vince Staples Show was such a surprise—Vince can really do it all.

Fallout was another standout. I wasn't into the games much, but that show was a banger. The acting, visuals, and writing were top-tier. Live-action Avatar had its issues (shifting the story around unnecessarily), but I still enjoyed it. Season 2 will be a true test with Toph. I'm also waiting on Squid Game season 2 and the final season of Stranger Things. Invincible was good but way too short. And of course, The Boys season 4 was fantastic—my love for comic-based content knows no bounds. The Acolyte was a letdown outside of the action, but overall, there's still a lot to love in streaming right now.

Music

Music is the toughest subject for me to talk about, but it's also the most important art form in my life. Kendrick Lamar is my GOAT—my all-time favorite rapper. His ability to paint vivid pictures with his lyrics while maintaining high-level technical skill is unmatched. On the other hand, Drake is a rapper I feel hasn't been rapping at a high level for nearly a decade. I don't get the hype around albums like Views, which I think is one of the worst rap albums I've ever heard. While he still drops a hit now and then, the idea of Drake being the best rapper alive, let alone ever, is insane to me. Needless to say, when their rivalry peaked, I was happy to see Kendrick strategically pick Drake apart and out-rap him, as expected. Kendrick's pen is simply better—beyond the gimmicks and internet drama.

My favorite type of rap is raw, lyrical displays without the need for a hook, where two elite rappers are going back and forth over a hard-hitting beat. Family Ties by Kendrick and Baby Keem is, in my opinion, the best rap song ever. I love those slow, gritty, cinematic beats, the kind that my favorite producer, The Alchemist, excels at. Another one of my top 3 guys, Earl Sweatshirt, often raps over that kind of production as well. Hip hop is definitely my favorite genre, but I have a deep love for alternative music too.

Billie Eilish is an artist who can do no wrong in my eyes. Her style, vocals, and innovation keep me hooked, and I expect Grammy-level output every time she drops. In the rap world, ScHoolboy Q's new album is fire. He redeemed himself after CrasH Talk, which I think was the only bad album from the Black Hippy crew. Other standout albums this year came from Vince Staples, Jerreau, and Lucki. I'm also hyped for upcoming projects from Ab-Soul and Tyler, the Creator.

I don't really care about Kendrick headlining the Super Bowl, but I'm praying for a new album rollout soon. The only other rapper I'm really waiting to hear from is Baby Keem—he's got next.
